# SaaS Validation Scorecard

## Purpose

Determine whether a customer problem is strong enough to justify building a SaaS MVP.

The goal is to validate a painful, frequent, expensive problem before investing significant development time.

---

## Scoring

Each category receives a score from 1–5.

### Pain Severity

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Barely noticeable |
| 2 | Minor annoyance |
| 3 | Meaningful inconvenience |
| 4 | Significant business problem |
| 5 | Critical problem |

### Frequency

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Rarely |
| 2 | Monthly |
| 3 | Weekly |
| 4 | Several times per week |
| 5 | Daily or multiple times per day |

### Time Cost

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Less than 15 minutes/week |
| 2 | 15–60 minutes/week |
| 3 | 1–3 hours/week |
| 4 | 3–5 hours/week |
| 5 | More than 5 hours/week |

### Existing Workaround

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Easy existing solution |
| 2 | Minor manual workaround |
| 3 | Several manual steps |
| 4 | Complicated or unreliable workaround |
| 5 | No satisfactory solution |

### Willingness to Pay

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Would not pay |
| 2 | Interested but unlikely to pay |
| 3 | Might pay |
| 4 | Would seriously consider paying |
| 5 | Actively looking for a paid solution |

---

## Total Score

Maximum: 25

### 21–25 — Strong Opportunity

Proceed toward MVP definition.

### 16–20 — Promising

Conduct additional interviews and refine the problem.

### 11–15 — Weak

Continue research but do not build yet.

### 5–10 — Poor

Abandon or significantly change the hypothesis.

---

## Validation Requirements

Do not build the MVP based on a single customer.

Target:

- 5+ customer interviews
- 3+ customers reporting the same core problem
- Average validation score of at least 18/25
- Evidence of significant time or financial cost
- Existing workaround identified
- Evidence of willingness to pay

---

## Evidence Rules

Customer statements must be recorded accurately.

Do not:

- Invent customer problems
- Lead customers toward desired answers
- Suggest features during discovery
- Treat compliments as buying signals
- Treat "that sounds useful" as willingness to pay

Strong evidence includes:

- Existing spending
- Significant recurring time loss
- Repeated operational mistakes
- Lost revenue
- Customer complaints caused by the problem
- Existing attempts to solve the problem
- Explicit willingness to pay

---

## Build Decision

### BUILD

Requirements:

- Repeated problem
- Significant pain
- Frequent occurrence
- Existing workaround
- Evidence of willingness to pay

### MODIFY

Requirements:

- Problem exists
- Pain is moderate
- Current hypothesis does not fully address it

Conduct additional interviews.

### ABANDON

Requirements:

- Problem is weak
- Problem is infrequent
- Customers already have satisfactory solutions
- No willingness to pay

Return to customer discovery and identify another problem.
