## Customer Workflow

### Customer Intake

How customers contact the business:

Customers contact the business by phone and text.

Information collected:

Basic customer and job information is collected during the initial contact.

### Photos and Documents

How photos/videos are received:

Customers send photos through text messages.

How they are stored:

Photos are managed through the existing messaging workflow.

### Estimates

How estimates are created:

Estimates are created in QuickBooks.

How estimates are sent:

Estimates are sent to customers through the existing QuickBooks workflow.

How pending estimates are tracked:

Pending estimates are tracked manually and sometimes get lost.

## Scheduling

How jobs are scheduled:

Jobs are scheduled using a paper calendar.

How rescheduling is handled:

Rescheduling is handled manually.

## Billing

How invoices are created:

Invoices are created in QuickBooks.

How payments are tracked:

Payments are tracked through QuickBooks, with manual follow-up for unpaid invoices.

## Current Tools

Software currently used:

QuickBooks and Google Sheets.

Other tools:

Phone, text messaging, paper calendar, and personal notes.

## Pain Discovery

Biggest administrative pain:

Following up on unpaid invoices.

Frequency:

Approximately 3–5 unpaid-invoice follow-ups per week.

Time consumed:

Approximately 1 hour per week specifically following up on payments, with approximately 3 hours per week spent on administrative work overall.

Current workaround:

Google Sheets, personal notes, manual reminders, and QuickBooks.

What happens when the problem occurs:

Unpaid invoices require manual follow-up, and pending estimates can sometimes be missed.

## Customer Language

Important statements:

- Unpaid invoices require regular follow-up.
- Pending estimates sometimes get lost.
- Multiple tools are used to manage different parts of the workflow.
- Automated customer reminders would be useful.

## Buying Signals

Existing spending:

The business already pays for and uses QuickBooks.

Urgency:

The business has recurring administrative work and outstanding invoices that require follow-up.

Interest in a better solution:

The strongest interest is in invoice follow-up, estimate tracking, and scheduling. The business would consider paying for a solution that saves administrative time and improves payment collection.

## Product Opportunity

Potential problem worth solving:

Small home-service businesses may need a simpler way to track estimates, scheduled jobs, completed work, and unpaid invoices without managing several disconnected tools.

Potential MVP feature:

Automated customer reminders combined with estimate tracking, scheduling, and invoice follow-up.
